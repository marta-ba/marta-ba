The business problem

A company ran a digital advertising campaign and wants to know two things: Did it work? And if it did, how much of the result was actually caused by the ads — and how much would have happened anyway?

This is the question most marketing dashboards fail to answer. Correlation between ad exposure and conversion is easy to find. Causation is harder — and far more valuable.

What the analysis found

  • The A/B framework compared users exposed to ads vs. users shown a neutral public service announcement — isolating the causal effect of advertising from background conversion behaviour.
  • High ad exposure did not always correlate with higher conversion — diminishing returns were identified above a certain frequency threshold.
  • Temporal patterns revealed specific time windows where ad delivery was significantly more effective, suggesting budget could be concentrated rather than spread evenly.
  • Outlier users with extreme ad exposure were segmented separately — their behaviour would have distorted aggregate results if left in.

What I would recommend to the business

Scale the campaign only if statistical significance AND positive lift are confirmed simultaneously. A result that is statistically significant but economically trivial doesn't justify budget. A result that looks large but doesn't pass the significance threshold is noise.

Cap ad frequency at the point where diminishing returns begin — reinvest that budget in high-performing time windows identified in the temporal analysis. This alone could improve ROAS without increasing total spend.

Why causal inference matters
A user who converts after seeing 10 ads might have converted anyway. The control group (PSA) isolates the counterfactual: what would have happened without the campaign?
Key metrics
Conversion rate by group · Ad exposure distribution · Temporal conversion patterns · Statistical significance (p-value, confidence interval)
Dataset
User-level observations with group assignment (ad vs PSA), conversion outcome, total ads seen, and time-of-exposure metadata.
