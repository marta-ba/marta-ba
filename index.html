The problem

An online fashion retailer was seeing traffic grow month-over-month, but conversion rate remained stuck at 11%. The marketing team was spending heavily on acquisition, but 89% of visitors were leaving without purchasing.

The question: Could we identify high-intent visitors before they left, and trigger interventions (discount codes, chat nudges, exit-intent popups) at precisely the right moment?

This required a model that could:

  • Predict purchase intent from session behaviour alone
  • Work in real time (not post-session analysis)
  • Handle severe class imbalance (11% buyers vs 89% non-buyers)

Methodology

1. Data preparation

Worked with 12,330 e-commerce sessions from a UCI dataset. Features included:

  • Behavioural: Page views, bounce rates, exit rates, time on page
  • Temporal: Month, weekend/weekday, proximity to special days
  • Technical: Browser, OS, region, traffic type

2. Handling class imbalance

The dataset had a severe imbalance (85% non-buyers). I used SMOTE (Synthetic Minority Oversampling) to generate synthetic buyer examples, balancing the training set without losing real patterns.

3. Model selection

Tested multiple algorithms:

  • Logistic Regression (baseline)
  • Decision Tree
  • Random Forest (final choice)
  • Gradient Boosting

Random Forest performed best, achieving 87% accuracy and 78% recall on buyers — meaning it caught 78% of actual purchasers.

Key findings

Feature importance: ProductRelated_Duration and PageValues were the strongest predictors of purchase intent.
ROC curve showing model performance. AUC = 0.91, indicating excellent discrimination between buyers and non-buyers.

The model revealed that time spent on product pages was the single strongest predictor of purchase — more important than traffic source, device type, or even number of pages viewed.

Business impact

Projected revenue uplift: +€11,700 per period
By targeting high-intent users identified by the model with a 10% discount offer, we projected a conversion lift of 2.5 percentage points (from 11% to 13.5%). At an average order value of €95, this represents significant incremental revenue with minimal acquisition cost.

Recommendations delivered to stakeholders:

  1. Real-time scoring: Integrate model into website backend to score visitors during session
  2. Tiered interventions: Show different offers based on intent score (high/medium/low)
  3. A/B testing framework: Measure actual lift vs. control group before full rollout

The project demonstrated that machine learning could move from post-hoc analysis to real-time business decision-making — a shift the marketing team hadn't considered before.

What I learned

Class imbalance is the norm, not the exception. Real business problems rarely have balanced datasets. Learning to use SMOTE, weighted loss functions, and recall-focused metrics was critical.

Model accuracy isn't the goal — business value is. A model with 85% accuracy that catches only 50% of buyers is useless. Optimising for recall (catching actual buyers) was more important than overall accuracy.

Feature engineering > algorithm choice. Random Forest only outperformed simpler models after I engineered interaction features (e.g., PageValues × ProductRelated_Duration).
