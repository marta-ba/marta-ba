Hotel reservations ETL — do city events actually move bookings?

End-to-end ETL on hotel reservation data — customers, hotels, bookings and pricing — enriched with open-data event APIs from the Madrid Community to test whether city events have a measurable impact on demand and revenue. Loaded into PostgreSQL for SQL-driven analysis and EDA.

The problem

Hotel reservation data was scattered across raw exports, partially transformed files and ad-hoc spreadsheets. There was no clean way to ask basic business questions: who are our top spenders, how does revenue compare against competitors, and — most importantly — do city events actually drive bookings, or is it just a feeling?

Methodology

1. Extract

  • Raw reservation files from multiple sources (customers, hotels, reservations, pricing)
  • Event data pulled from Madrid Community open-data APIs

2. Transform

  • Cleaning, deduplication and standardisation in Python (pandas, numpy)
  • Unification of raw, transformed and enriched files into a single schema
  • Feature engineering: event flags, seasonality, revenue per booking

3. Load

  • Structured load into PostgreSQL via psycopg2
  • Relational model linking customers · reservations · hotels · events

4. Analyse

  • SQL queries to extract KPIs and answer business questions directly from the warehouse
  • Exploratory data analysis and visualisation in matplotlib / seaborn
Python 3.8+ pandas numpy psycopg2 PostgreSQL REST APIs matplotlib · seaborn

Key insights

Reservations plotted against major Madrid events — visualising the lift around peak periods.
  • Hotel inventory: total hotels in scope, queried directly from the warehouse
  • Reservations: volume and seasonality patterns over time
  • Top spenders: top 10 customers by lifetime spend
  • Competitive revenue: our brand vs competitor hotels
  • Event impact: peak events show a measurable effect on reservations — useful signal for pricing and marketing

Next steps

  • Enhance the ETL pipeline with additional data sources (loyalty, channel mix, weather)
  • Apply machine-learning models for revenue and demand forecasting
  • Integrate external factors — holidays, promotions — for deeper, more actionable insights

Why it matters

A clean, queryable warehouse turns one-off questions into reusable answers. Once the events layer is in place, marketing and revenue management can stop guessing and start pricing around real demand signals.
