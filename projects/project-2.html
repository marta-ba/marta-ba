The problem

The marketing team was reporting "ad campaigns increased conversions by 25%" — but conversions had been growing organically anyway. How much lift was actually caused by the ads?

The team needed a clean, defensible methodology to separate signal from noise — one that wouldn't be dismissed by finance or product leadership.

Methodology

1. Designing the test

  • Defined treatment and control groups with proper randomisation
  • Set sample size based on minimum detectable effect (MDE)
  • Pre-registered hypotheses to avoid p-hacking

2. Statistical testing

Two-sample t-tests for continuous metrics, chi-square for categorical. Validated assumptions (normality, equal variance) before drawing conclusions.

3. Causal framing

Framed every conclusion in terms of counterfactuals: "What would have happened without the ad?" — not "what correlates with the ad?"

Business impact

Reframed how the team measured marketing ROI

The framework became the default standard for evaluating new campaigns. Two campaigns that had been celebrated as "successes" turned out to have zero statistically significant lift — saving budget that was reallocated to channels with measurable impact.
